# INC-0016 — Partner email degradation: 35 % of eGift orders fail at `send_email` (Day 14, game day 1, fault 1)

A hand edit of the egift Deployment (`EMAIL_FAIL_RATE=0.35`, revision 8, no change-cause,
no pipeline run, no annotation) made a third of corporate eGift orders fail at the email
step. Activation — the dependency every previous egift incident came from — was healthy
throughout. The platform said so within two minutes; the *why* had to be reconstructed by
hand, because nothing on the platform records a change that bypasses the pipeline.

## What happened

At 14:44 the egift Deployment was edited by hand; the rollout replaced both pods within a
minute and the error rate went from 1 % to 35 % on the next order. The alert has a 2-minute
window and `for: 2m`, so it fired at 14:48:35; the overview showed the row red thirty
seconds earlier. The ticket arrived with the answer to "what" already on it — the email
step, 77 % of failures, no deploy — and the remediator correctly refused to touch it.

What the ticket could not say was *why*, because the deploy collector reads Grafana
annotations, and only the pipeline writes those. The responder got there through the pod
list (egift pods minutes old while everything else was hours), `rollout history` (revision
8, `CHANGE-CAUSE <none>`), and the revision's environment (`EMAIL_FAIL_RATE=0.35`). Seven
minutes from alert to root cause, five of them spent on that reconstruction. Meanwhile the
copilot, asked whether activation was affected, got the activation half right and the
egift half wrong (it took a healthy `send_email` *latency* as evidence the step was not
failing — Eval 6c).

Reverting the env took one command; the rollout took thirty seconds; the alert took three
more minutes to agree.

## What went well / badly

Well: detected by alert; ticket in 49 s; right hypothesis in 2 min; correct non-action by
the remediator; the responder wrote "do NOT assume activation" before looking, and did not.
Badly: a change outside the pipeline is invisible to the enrichment — the most expensive
five minutes of the incident; the copilot's wrong conclusion; two invented commands in the
hypothesis; a failed open draft; the Tempo trace check was skipped.
