## What happened

Four minutes after the latency fault, settlement began crashing. The first run after the
change pushed a 0 and exited 1, so `SettlementZeroRecords` opened the ticket at 20:41:46Z as
tier 3; when `SettlementJobFailed` joined two minutes later the `settlement-crash` signature
matched within sixteen seconds and the remediator re-ran the job (failed, the mode was
still set), retried once after 180 s (failed), and stopped, saying a second failure is a
human's problem. The "vendor" reset the mode at 20:50:19Z. The next cron run at 20:55Z was
clean; the alerts drained on their windows; the platform resolved the ticket at 21:05:46Z with
a resolution draft attached. In between, at 21:02:02Z, the remediator's cooldown expired
while `SettlementJobFailed` was still firing for the *old* failed jobs and it re-ran a
settlement that had been healthy for seven minutes — successfully and harmlessly, but on a
stale signal.

## What went well / badly

Well: the whole tier-1 policy executed as written and readable on one timeline — attempt,
bounded retry, cooldown, the words "a human's problem"; the vendor fix needed no operator
action; the ticket closed itself. Badly, and both are the platform misleading itself: (1)
**kb-004's own discriminator is wrong** — "a crash pushes nothing; compare kb-005 which pushes
0" — a crash *does* push 0 since Day 8, so the KB steered the hypothesis to the quiet entry
for the loud fault; and the alert pair (`ZeroRecords`, then `JobFailed`) is *identical* for the
two patterns — only the job's last log line tells them apart, and it is not in the hypothesis' input; (2) the remediator's
`detect` reads the alert, not the service — a failed-job alert outlives recovery by up to 15
minutes, and the signature re-ran on that debris after its cooldown.

## Follow-ups
- [x] kb-004 and kb-005: discriminator corrected — since Day 8 a crash *and* a strict silent run both push `records=0, status=0` and both open on `ZeroRecords` then `JobFailed`; the metrics cannot tell them apart, **the job's last log line can** (`db_unreachable`/exit 1 vs `refusing to report success`/exit 2). INC-0021 added to both `learned_from`
- [ ] hypothesis input for settlement tickets: the newest job's last log line (the logs collector returns reason histograms; a crash's `db_unreachable` is one row, easy to add) — the one fact that discriminates
- [ ] `settlement-crash` `detect`: before re-running, check `time() - settlement_last_success_timestamp < 600` → "already recovered, not acting" (0022-c)
- [ ] hypothesis: re-draft (or append) when a *new alert name* joins an open ticket — the settlement ticket's only hypothesis predates the alert that identifies the fault
- [ ] the 19 seconds: a vendor fix and a cron tick will race in production too; the runbook line is "after the fix, expect one more failure if a run was already cut"
