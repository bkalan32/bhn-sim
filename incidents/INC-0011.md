## Translation tax, measured
Q1 took the copilot 8 tool calls and **22 seconds** end to end, and it had the right cause
**31 seconds before the first alert fired** — 157 s after the fault. On Day 3 the same three
lookups took roughly ten minutes, most of it writing the Splunk search. On Day 10 the bot did
them in one second, but only once a ticket opened (188 + 29 s after the fault). The copilot
does them **on demand, for any question**, which is the difference between a triage bot and an
assistant — and it means the question "is something wrong?" can now be answered before
detection, if someone thinks to ask.

## What it got wrong
The invented inventory is gone: every kubectl, PromQL and SPL the copilot ran was real. What
replaced it is subtler — a **causal story around real evidence**. It saw `FRAUD_SVC_DOWN: true`
and pods three minutes old (both true, both more than the bot could see) and wrote "build 20
deployed 3 minutes ago, leftover from the revert" (false: build 20 shipped hours earlier; the
pods restarted because the env changed). Then it carried that cause into the stakeholder
update, with jargon. Root cause: **no "what changed" tool** on the menu — the Grafana
annotation lookup the bot has since Day 10 was not available to the copilot, so it inferred a
deploy from pod age. Fixed the same hour: `recent_deploys` tool, pod-age caveat in the kubectl
description, and a prompt rule — *report what the tool shows; do not narrate how it came to
be*. More tools, more evidence, more rope.
