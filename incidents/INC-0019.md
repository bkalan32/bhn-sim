## What the KB changed, and what it did not

It changed the *text of the diagnosis* and nothing else. Section 6 reads like a senior
engineer's handover: which entry, which incidents, which of its checks the evidence already
settles, which remain, what the team did last time and at what tier. The checks are the
entry's, so they are real (`histogram_quantile` on the real bucket series, the real Splunk
field, the lab's real knob) — Eval 3's invented inventory did not recur, and the confidence
went from medium to high on the same three sources because the model had something to
compare them against. The runner-up entry did the second job the retrieval was designed for:
the look-alike was named and dismissed on its documented discriminator.

It did not change detection (2 m 59 s, the same `for:` windows as every run), the ticket
latency (20 s), the remediator's decision (tier 3 either way), or the resolution shape (the
windows again). The KB is input to one prompt; the platform around it is unchanged. That is
the honest size of "engineering knowledge systems": a better answer at the same speed, not a
faster incident.

Also today: the copilot, asked the same question with no incident open, searched the KB,
cited kb-001 and then *disagreed with the question* on live evidence (2.4 % errors, zero
timeouts in 10 min, p95 normal): "known pattern, not active". A KB entry is a prior, not a
verdict, and both callers treated it that way.
