## What the drafts got right, and what they could not know
Graded in `docs/ai-eval.md` Eval 2. The open draft got every fact right — alert, service,
start time, the 29-second lag to the ticket, the runbook verbatim — and could not name a
cause because the record had none; it did not invent one. It did invent, for the third
record running, that "the engineering team is actively investigating": at 18:34:09 nobody
had done anything yet. The resolution draft is the first **usable** draft of the day: zero
invented claims, an eight-entry timeline with both notes quoted verbatim, and Root cause
filled *and cited* — "per responder observation in Splunk". Two things for a human's pen:
the note said *suspect* and the draft says *was caused by*; and the burn-rate alert's 14.4×
threshold was presented as a measurement next to the real 21×.

## The comparison with Eval 1 (same fault, no notes)
Root cause went from *not yet known* to filled-and-attributed. Timeline went from 4 entries
to 8. Invented claims at close went from 1 to 0. Nothing about the model or the prompt
changed; two lines a human typed did. Effort goes to the scribe, not the prompt.

## Division of labour
Humans observe and decide; the AI compiles and drafts. The stakeholder update is the
clearest case: a responder at minute 3 of an outage should not be composing prose for
leadership, and leadership should not be reading alert names. A draft that is 90% right in
15 seconds, reviewed by a human, beats a perfect one written by hand at minute 20.
