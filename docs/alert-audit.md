# Alert audit — every rule interrogated, with data (Day 18, Part A)

The rule that is most of the alert-fatigue literature, and the README's line:
**every page must be actionable and urgent; everything else is a ticket or a graph.**

Four questions per rule (the standard interrogation), answered next to two numbers this
lab can produce and most teams cannot: **hours firing in the last 10 days** (Prometheus's
own `ALERTS` series) and **tickets** — how many of the nineteen incidents on the bot carried
this alert. An alert that has fired for 200 hours and ticketed zero times is not an alert; an
alert that has fired for 0 hours in ten days of drills may be a blind spot or may be fine —
the column *why* says which.

- **Actionable?** When it fires, is there something a responder should *do*? No action = a log
  line with anxiety attached.
- **Urgent?** page (a human now) · ticket (soon) · dashboard (informational).
- **Symptom or cause?** Symptom alerts (error rate, staleness, absence) catch unknown
  failures; cause alerts (CPU, restarts) fire for things users never feel — page on symptoms,
  ticket on the few causes that are always worth a "why".
- **Has it fired usefully?** The *tickets* column and the incident write-ups it appears in.

The table is generated by `scripts/180-alert-audit.sh` (data columns) with the verdicts from
its judgment map — edit the map when you disagree, re-run, and the doc and the code agree.

## Decisions (each with its reason — alert changes without recorded rationale are how the next person re-adds the noise)

**A1 — kind's four permanent false positives are removed at the scrape, not routed.**
`KubeScheduler/KubeControllerManager/KubeProxyInstanceUnreachable`, `etcdInsufficientMembers`,
`etcdMembersDown` (and their `TargetDown` shadow: **117 of 240 hours** red) have fired since Day 1: kind binds those components to 127.0.0.1, the
chart's scrapes cannot succeed, and an alert that has fired for eighteen days is an alert
nobody reads — Day 14 found the scheduler's **70 real restarts** hidden behind its fake
"Down". The PDF's lever is routing; for a rule about a component you *cannot scrape* the
chart's own switch (`kubeScheduler.enabled: false` …, the same four the EKS overlay uses)
is more honest: no scrape, no rule, no lie, and `terraform plan` stays clean. Their real
health now comes from `PlatformPodRestarting` (kube-state-metrics can see them).

**A2 — `ActivationHighLatency` (static p95 > 500 ms) is removed; `ActivationLatencyBudgetBurn` replaces it.**
It fired in every fraud drill at p95 0.48 s — the fail-fast timeout cap — and said nothing
the error-rate alert had not already said. The latency SLO from Day 5 (99 % under 300 ms)
already had an SLI and never had an alert; it now has the same two-window burn-rate shape
as availability, as a **warning** (a ticket: slow-but-completing is not a page). It joins the
activation ticket when one is open (`group_by: service`), so it never makes a second one.

**A3 — `ActivationErrorBudgetBurnFast` keeps its severity and loses its `for: 2m`.**
Twice on Day 17 it fired *after* the fix (3 m 43 s after, on INC-0019) and once opened a
second ticket for an incident that was over. The 1 h window needs ~4.3 min of a total outage
before it crosses 14.4×; the 5 m window then needs ~4.6 min after the fix to drop below it;
`for: 2m` on top delayed the whole thing past the recovery. The two windows *are* the
persistence check (that is the point of the design); a `for:` is double-counting. It also
stays a page next to `ActivationHighErrorRate` — belt and braces during the lab, because
HighErrorRate is the 2-minute catastrophe alert and BurnFast is the one that would catch a
30 % outage HighErrorRate's 10 % threshold also catches but a 5 % one it would not.
Decision recorded; revisit when the two have disagreed once.

**A4 — chart noise is routed to `null` explicitly.** `severity =~ "info|none"` and the
named cause-level rules that are not actionable on a laptop (`CPUThrottlingHigh` — we set
requests low on purpose; `Kube{Memory,CPU}Overcommit` — one node; `NodeClockNotSynchronising`
— WSL after sleep). Routed, not deleted (drift, Day 13), and the routes sit *above* the
ticket route so a demoted alert that also carries a `service` label still goes to null —
`181` tests exactly that label set.

**A5 — `IncidentBotDown` was delivered only to the bot.** Day 17 B10: the bot was down
twenty minutes and nothing, anywhere, said so. The remediator receives the same webhook and
now has a tier-1 signature for it: confirm `/healthz` really does not answer, `rollout
restart`, wait for ready, write the result — on the timeline the moment the bot is back. (A
company routes it to a pager; the lab has a remediator.)

**A6 — the alert the audit found missing: `PlatformPodRestarting`.** Day 14's scheduler
(70) and otel collector (112) and Day 17's Prometheus (47) restarted for a week or more
with no alert. Restarts are the one cause-level signal that is cheap, specific and always
actionable ("why?"). Warning, `service=platform`, 3+ restarts in an hour for 5 minutes,
platform namespaces only — the bot gets a ticket, the remediator has no signature for it
(tier 3, a human asks why).

**Kept as is, on purpose:** `ActivationHighErrorRate`, the three settlement alerts, the three
egift alerts, `ActivationNoTraffic`, `PaymentsPodCrashLooping`, `RemediatorDown`, and the
chart's `Prometheus*`/`Alertmanager*` self-monitoring — each has a row above saying why.
